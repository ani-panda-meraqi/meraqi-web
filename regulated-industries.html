AI design and media that runs inside your own network

Meraqi turns plain language into fully editable, multi-layer designs and video. It runs where your data already lives, on your intranet, your private data center, your government cloud or a server room you can walk into, so protected health information, controlled technical data and unreleased material never cross your boundary and never train a model.

The compliance question is a network question

Every mainstream AI design tool is a public website. To use one, your team uploads the asset to a third party's infrastructure. That might be a patient photograph, an unreleased product render, a technical drawing. There it is processed by models you do not control, retained under a policy you did not write, and handled by subprocessors you did not select.

For most companies that is a procurement conversation. For a hospital marketing team, a broker-dealer, an agency on a federal contract or a defense supplier, it is a control failure, and no amount of vendor assurance changes where the bytes went.

If the content never crosses your network boundary, most of the compliance argument never has to be made.

Meraqi is built to run on either side of that boundary. Deployed to your intranet, the models, the rendering pipeline and the designs you generate all sit inside your own perimeter. There is no upload step, no third-party model API call, and no telemetry carrying content out.

How deployment maps to obligations

This describes what the architecture provides. It is not a claim of certification. See what we don't claim below.

ObligationThe usual problem with public websitesWhat running it on your network changes
HIPAA Uploading imagery or copy containing PHI makes the vendor a business associate, requiring a BAA, subprocessor review and breach-notification terms. PHI never reaches AiOne. No business-associate relationship is created, so there is no BAA to negotiate and no external breach surface to assess.
CMMC · CUI Controlled Unclassified Information processed in a commercial multi-tenant cloud falls outside most enclave boundaries. Meraqi runs inside your existing enclave as another containerized workload, under controls you have already had assessed.
ITAR · EAR Transmitting technical data to a provider whose staff or infrastructure may be non-US is a potential export. Nothing is transmitted. Technical data stays on hardware inside a boundary you control, in a facility you choose.
Data residency Cross-border transfer, subprocessor chains, and retention terms you cannot alter. The data never moves. Residency is wherever you put the server, and retention is whatever your own policy says.
Model training Many providers reserve broad rights to train on customer content, sometimes behind an opt-out rather than an opt-in. Weights are static and local. There is no pipeline from your content back to any model. That is a matter of architecture, not of policy.

Air-gapped deployments arrive on a drive

A network with no egress cannot download anything, which is rather the point of it. So an air-gapped Meraqi ships as a single NVMe M.2 drive carrying the platform, the model weights and everything needed to stand it up inside the enclave. Nothing is fetched at install time, nothing phones home afterwards, and no step quietly assumes a connection.

Air-gapped delivery is built to order as part of an enterprise engagement rather than a download you configure yourself, because every enclave has its own rules about media, and pretending otherwise would waste your time and ours. Verification, government furnished media, and re-imaging before the boundary are all things we agree with you up front.

Updates arrive the same way, on your schedule, and they update the software, not your data.

Everything that happens in Meraqi stays in Meraqi

Containment is only half the requirement. The other half is being able to prove it. An auditor does not accept an architecture diagram as evidence, and neither does an incident review at two in the morning.

Every action taken in the system is written to an append-only audit record that lives on your deployment alongside everything else, so your audit trail is not sitting in a vendor's database behind a support ticket.

RecordedWhat the audit record contains
WhoThe identity and role behind every action, tied to the account that performed it.
WhatThe action and its object. Prompt submitted, design generated, layer edited, version restored, design exported, design deleted.
WhenEvery object in the system carries a UUIDv7, which is time-ordered by construction, so a sequence can be reconstructed exactly rather than approximately. The v1, v2, v3 you see in the interface are labels over those identifiers.
WhereTwo things. Where the request came from, and the path of the content itself, meaning which source assets were drawn on, which output they produced, and where that output was exported to.
HowThe interface used, and the generation job behind the output. Each design traces to its job, and the job records the model and version that ran.

Every user has their own design library, and nothing is overwritten

Each user works in a library of their own, holding every design they have generated, images and video alike, together with every version of each one. Users do not see one another's work unless it has been deliberately shared. That is how minimum necessary access under HIPAA, need to know handling for controlled information, and information barriers between deal teams get enforced rather than merely intended.

Versions accumulate instead of replacing one another. The design that was live last March is still there exactly as it was, which is the difference between answering a complaint and reconstructing an answer to one. Offboarding a user, placing a legal hold and satisfying an erasure request all operate against that boundary rather than as a search across shared storage you then have to prove was exhaustive.

Lineage runs in both directions

Events on their own tell you what happened. Lineage tells you what it touched. Every design records the assets it was built from and the outputs it went on to produce, so the chain running from source material through generation to export is one connected record rather than a scatter of unrelated log lines.

That makes the expensive question cheap. If a source file turns out to have carried protected health information, or a licensed image is withdrawn, or legal pulls a campaign after it has shipped, you can ask what else is downstream of this one file and get a complete answer. Ask a public design website the same question and there is no answer available at any price, because it never recorded which upload fed which output.

The record is yours

Audit records and lineage live on your deployment, queryable there, under your retention. Nothing is sampled, summarized or held back behind a higher pricing tier, which is the usual way audit data becomes an upsell.

Built on open models, not an API wrapper

Meraqi runs open-weight models on its own inference stack. That matters for three reasons beyond privacy.

  • No dependency on a third-party API. Your creative pipeline cannot be broken by someone else's rate limit, deprecation notice or price change.
  • Auditability. You can name the model, the version and the license under which it is used, which is the first question any serious procurement review asks.
  • Output is editable, not final. Meraqi produces multi-layer designs you can open and change, not flat images you have to regenerate. In a regulated setting that matters more than elsewhere, because legal review marks up copy and you need to change that line without re-rolling the whole design.

What we don't claim

Regulated buyers are used to vendors who blur this line. We would rather be precise, because you will find out at diligence anyway.

  • Meraqi is not "HIPAA certified." No software is, because HIPAA has no certification regime. Compliance is a property of your organization and your configuration. What we provide is an architecture that keeps PHI inside your boundary so the obligation stays yours to satisfy rather than ours to promise.
  • We do not hold SOC 2 or FedRAMP authorization today. AiOne LLC is an early-stage company and we will say so plainly rather than imply otherwise.
  • Export to your SIEM is on the roadmap, not shipped. Audit records are queryable on your deployment today. Integrations with Splunk, Sentinel, Elastic and syslog are planned and we will not pretend they exist.
  • Model provenance is recorded per generation job, not yet stamped on each design. Lineage joins the two, so the answer is reachable, but the per-design stamp is still to come.
  • Signed release media is intended, not yet in place. We want a repeatable signed build with hashes published away from the drive. Until that exists, verification is something we agree with you per engagement rather than a process we can point at.
  • Self-hosting does not make you compliant by itself. Your access controls, audit review, retention schedule and staff training still do the work. Meraqi removes one category of risk, which is third-party data handling. It does not remove the rest.
  • We are pre-launch. Meraqi is in active development with a beta program. If you need a production deployment on a fixed date, tell us the date before you invest time in evaluation.
