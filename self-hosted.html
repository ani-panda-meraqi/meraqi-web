Containers · Open weights · Your network

Self-hosted AI design platform

Meraqi turns plain language into fully editable, multi-layer designs, images and video. You can run the whole thing on your own network. The models sit on your disk, your work stays in your storage, and nothing is uploaded to anyone in order to generate anything.

What self-hosted actually means here

The phrase gets stretched. Plenty of tools describe themselves as self-hosted when what they mean is that you host a thin front end which then calls someone else's inference API. Your prompt still leaves the building, and the bill still scales with a token counter you cannot see.

Meraqi is not that. The inference runs locally. Model weights are pulled once, staged on your disk, and served from your own GPU. With the weights in place the platform will run with outbound network access blocked entirely, which is something you can verify with a firewall rule rather than a trust exercise.

If your internet is down and it still works, it was self-hosted. If it stops, it was not.

Two places it can run

Our service, over the internet

Sign up and sign in at meraqi.ai. It is one multi-tenant service that individuals and businesses all share, with nothing to provision. The right answer if you would rather not own GPUs or think about containers.

Creators · Teams · Agencies

Your network, whatever that means to you

A cloud direct connect, a government cloud, a private data center, an in-office server room, or a box under someone's desk. If it sits inside your perimeter, it qualifies. At the far end is a deployment with no egress at all.

Homelabs · Studios · Regulated teams

There is no confusing middle tier. Either the content travels to us or it does not. If you want the second option and have a reasonably current GPU, tell us what you are running and we will say honestly whether it will work before you spend an evening on it.

The stack, stated plainly

Deployment is containerized. The whole runtime is declared in one place, and that declaration is the single source of truth for what gets started.

  • An inference server for the language side, serving the quantized open-weight model that handles prompt interpretation and the design treatment.
  • A diffusion runtime for image generation, driven by a versioned workflow catalog so the exact graph that produced an image is recorded rather than implied.
  • A video pipeline built on an open-weight video model.
  • The editor, which is where the layered output lands and stays editable rather than arriving as a flat render.

Every service binds to loopback by default. Nothing is exposed on a public interface unless you choose to put it there, and no component phones home for license checks during generation.

Model choices live in a deployment catalog that comes with the platform, rather than being hidden in the application, so you can see which weights are in play, swap them for others you prefer, and pin a version that does not move until you move it.

What running it yourself gets you

The model does not change underneath you

Public websites update their models when it suits them, and you are not told when the swap happens. Here the weights are a file on your disk, and they change when you replace them. Updates arrive the same way, on your schedule, and they update the software, not your data.

Nothing is uploaded to generate anything

Source material, prompts, brand assets and output all stay in your storage. There is no upload step to audit, no retention policy to read, and no subprocessor list to review, because there is no third party in the path.

Cost stops tracking headcount

On your own hardware the marginal cost of another generation is electricity. Seat-based pricing exists because the vendor carries your compute. Once you carry it, that logic no longer applies to you.

Your history is a record, not a feature tier

Every object in the system carries a time-ordered UUIDv7, and the v1, v2, v3 you see in the interface are labels over those identifiers. Every asset keeps its versions and its lineage, back to the source material it was built from and forward to wherever it was exported. That is stored with everything else on your deployment rather than rented back to you as an audit add-on. The full treatment is on the regulated industries page.

Is Meraqi open source?

No, and it is worth saying that plainly rather than letting the word do work it has not earned.

Meraqi is commercial software that you self-host. The models it runs are open weight, the deployment is yours, the data is yours, and nothing reaches us. What you do not get is the platform source under an OSI-approved license or the right to fork it.

If open source is a hard requirement rather than shorthand for control, the honest answer is that Meraqi will not meet it, and we would rather tell you now than after an evaluation. Genuinely open tools exist for both design and image generation, with OSI-approved licenses, the right to fork, and real communities behind them. If the license is what matters most, that is where your time is best spent.

If what you actually want is control over where your content lives, which model runs, and when anything changes, self-hosted commercial software gets you all three without the license.

Questions we get asked

Is Meraqi a self-hosted AI image generator?

It does that, and more than that. Image generation is one capability alongside video and fully editable multi-layer design, with 3D on the roadmap. If you only want a self-hosted image generator there are simpler tools. Meraqi is for people who need the output to stay editable and to sit in a platform rather than a folder of renders.

Is Meraqi an open-source Canva alternative?

It is a self-hosted alternative rather than an open-source one. The platform is commercial software you run on your own network, built on open-weight models. If you need an OSI-approved license and the right to fork, Meraqi will not meet that requirement, and a genuinely open project is the better choice for you.

Who else offers a self-hosted generative AI design platform?

None that we have found, as of September 18, 2026, if you mean a finished design platform for a creative team, with editable multi-layer designs, images and video in one product, that runs entirely on your own network. The nearby options are different things. Most AI design and media tools are public websites that meter usage in credits, so self-hosting is not offered. Open-source image and video generators run locally for free. They produce renders rather than editable design documents and are yours to run and maintain, and some are self-hosted in name only because the part you run still calls someone else's models. A small number of enterprise vendors deploy generative models inside your environment, usually as an API for developers to build on. If you know of a platform we have missed, tell us and we will correct this answer. Whichever you evaluate, apply the same test. Block outbound access and see whether it still generates.

Does it need an internet connection to generate anything?

No. Weights are staged once, after which generation runs entirely locally and the platform will operate with outbound access blocked. That is verifiable with a firewall rule rather than taken on trust.

What if I do not have a GPU?

Then use our online service at meraqi.ai. Self-hosting means you supply the compute, and generative work of this kind needs a reasonably current GPU to be pleasant. We would rather tell you that than sell you a deployment that disappoints. Tell us what hardware you have and we will give you a straight answer about whether it is worth it.

Can I choose or swap the models?

Yes. Model choices live in a deployment catalog that comes with the platform, rather than being hidden in the application, so you can see which weights are in use, substitute others, and pin a version that stays put until you decide to change it.

Is my work used to train anything?

No. In a self-hosted deployment there is no mechanism by which it could be. The weights are local and static and nothing is transmitted off the system.

Does Meraqi train its own models?

Not at this time. Meraqi runs open-weight models made by others, and our own work is the platform that turns them into editable, consistent designs and video. We may train models of our own in the future. If we do, they will never learn from your content.

How does self-hosting affect pricing?

Once you supply the compute, the marginal cost of a generation is electricity rather than a per-seat fee. Licensing for self-hosted deployment is handled separately from the online plans. See pricing for the online plans, and talk to us for a self-hosted quote.
